title: "Writing a Great Paper"
---

How can one write a great paper? Identifying the best of the past is doable because of a variety of signals such as adoption into widely used systems or a high citation count. But determining whether what one is working on will be great is challenging. If working in a very hot area (e.g. [networking in the late 90s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble)) the competition will be fierce and the amount of work and talent required to produce a seminal work will be that much higher. However, researching well-established fields is even harder as the low hanging fruit will certainly have been pruned. The goal is to provide new conceptual insights, and two strategies could be to simply 'spray and pray' by producing so much literature that some of it is bound to be groundbreaking. Alternatively, one could spend additional effort to identify areas with low hanging fruit which are often ones that require a very rare combination of skills. Part of the work here is literature search, and the other part is building those skills. Regardless, the crux of both strategies is to work very, very hard.
